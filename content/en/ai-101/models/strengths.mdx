## Look at the task, not the slogan

People say AI “can do anything” mostly because it can hand back a paragraph or a block of code that looks finished. A tighter split is more useful: it is good at patterned work on material you already gave it, and it is not good at owning a judgment you have not stated.

Treat one request as putting material in and asking the model to rewrite, summarize, compare, or draft it under your rules. Specific material can be checked. “Make it better” leaves the standard for the model to guess.

## Four kinds of work it usually does well

- Find, group, and rewrite text you provided. For example, turn a stack trace into “what happened, a possible cause, and a next step.”
- Continue a format you showed. Given the way a function is written now, it is more likely to add a short piece in that style than to invent a new structure.
- List edges you left out. “What might this change miss?” is often more useful than “just finish it.”
- Draft something you can compare immediately: a note, a test case, a commit message.

These share one property: you can check them quickly. Output you cannot check only looks done.

### An example you can draw a circle around

Change a button from `Browse courses` to `Start learning`, and leave the destination alone. That fits a model, because one sentence can bound it and the page shows the result.

“Make the learning experience good” does not fit in one handoff. Whose standard, which pages may change, and what done means are still open. The model can offer ten ideas. It cannot pick the one change you will make this time.

## Decisions that stay with you

A model can propose. It cannot settle three things for you:

1. Whether this goal is the one to do now.
2. Which current behavior must stay as it is.
3. What counts as actually finished, rather than “it said it was finished.”

Write those three as short lines before you ask for a draft. Reverse the order and you will be choosing among paragraphs that all look plausible, instead of checking a task you already defined.

<Callout title="Good at it is not the same as unchecked" type="info">
Fast drafting only means drafting is cheap. Scope, facts, and safety still need a pass from you.
</Callout>

## Try it

Pick one small change you actually want this week and split it into two columns:

- Hand to AI: you can point at a file or a place in the UI, and you can check it in a few minutes.
- Keep for yourself: the goal is still vague, or a mistake would be hard to notice.

Keep only the smallest item in the first column for the later lessons. Do not start the rest. If both columns stay empty, the task is still a slogan. Turn it into a change you can see.

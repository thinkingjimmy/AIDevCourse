## The model is not counting the characters you see

On screen you count characters, words, or lines. A model counts tokens. One token may be a common word, part of a word, or a few Chinese characters, and the split depends on the model. The same paragraph can have different lengths in two models.

This is not pedantry. The window has a limit: only material that fits in this turn is material the model can actually use. Files that do not fit, earlier turns, and agreements you never pasted did not happen, as far as this request is concerned.

## What sits in the context window

The window is not the model’s long-term memory. It is the workbench for this request. It usually mixes:

- the prompt you wrote this turn
- system or project rules
- code, errors, and files you attached
- earlier turns in the conversation
- tool results, if a tool ran

Together they spend the window. Long rules, a long history, and a pasted repository can push the few relevant lines to the edge, or cut them off.

### Rough sizes are only estimates

| What you see | How to estimate it | What not to conclude |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A short prompt | Usually a small slice of the window | A short prompt does not mean the context is complete |
| One source file | Often larger than it feels | Do not paste the whole repo “to be safe” |
| A long conversation | Early turns may be dropped or summarized | Do not assume the first constraint is still there |
| Tool output | Logs and search results count too | Do not leave unrelated logs in the thread |

The table is an order of magnitude, not a formula. Whether you are over the limit is whatever the product shows or the error it returns. Products also place rules in different parts of the window, so “how much is left” is not a feeling.

## What happens when it does not fit

Near the limit, a product may quietly drop the oldest content, or it may reject the request. Both are risky: if content drops, you think the constraint is still in force; if the request is rejected, you have to choose what to cut.

> A fact that is not in the window will not be used reliably just because “the model should know.” The model will fill the gap with a common pattern, often in a confident tone.

Choosing context is editing: keep material you can check, and remove repetition and noise. A file name, a function name, and the original error take less room than a long paraphrase, and they are harder to distort.

<Callout title="Remove before you add" type="info">
If you are unsure a file belongs, leave it out. Ask what is missing, then add that small piece.
</Callout>

## Try it

Take material you were about to hand to an AI and mark how much comes from each row in the table. If the chat history or a whole file is clearly longer than the few lines this change needs, cut until you can say why each piece is present. Finish with one sentence: which piece is most likely to crowd out the real constraint.
